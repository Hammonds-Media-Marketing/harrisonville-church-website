# E-E-A-T Signals by Content Type

## Product Reviews

### Experience Signals (Critical)
- Original photos of the product in use — not press/stock images
- Specific test conditions documented (dates, environment, duration)
- Quantitative measurements (e.g., "held 47 lbs before the seam failed")
- Number of products tested/compared
- Disclosure of how long the reviewer has used the product

### Expertise Signals
- Author bio showing relevant domain knowledge or hobbyist depth
- Comparison to industry benchmarks or prior experience with similar products
- Discussion of edge cases, failure modes, or product limitations

### Trust Signals
- Affiliate disclosure prominently placed (FTC requirement)
- Clear methodology section
- Balanced pros/cons — not purely promotional
- Date of review + product version tested

### Authoritativeness Signals
- Author's other published reviews or coverage in relevant outlets
- Brand or site recognized in the product's category

## Blog Posts / Informational Articles

### Experience Signals
- First-person anecdotes with specific detail
- Original research, surveys, or data collection by the author
- Photos, screenshots, or documentation of the author's process
- Named examples from the author's own client/professional experience (with permission)

### Expertise Signals
- Depth that goes beyond surface-level summaries
- Addressing counterarguments or nuances
- Use of domain-specific language explained correctly
- Citing primary sources, not just other blog posts

### Trust Signals
- Clear author byline with linked bio
- Publication date and "last updated" date
- Source citations (inline links or footnotes)
- AI/automation disclosure if applicable

### Authoritativeness Signals
- Internal links to related in-depth content
- External backlinks from credible sources
- Author's broader publishing footprint (mentions, interviews, speaking)

## Landing Pages / Service Pages

### Experience Signals
- Case studies with named clients (or anonymized with permission)
- Before/after results with specific metrics
- Photos or video of actual work performed
- Team photos (real, not stock)

### Expertise Signals
- Specific methodology explained (not just "we do great work")
- Credentials, certifications, years of experience
- FAQs that address real objections and nuances

### Trust Signals
- Physical address and contact information visible
- Clear privacy policy and terms
- Client testimonials with full names and companies
- Third-party reviews (Google, Clutch, BBB)
- Money-back guarantees or clear refund policies disclosed

### Authoritativeness Signals
- Awards, recognitions, press mentions
- Industry memberships and certifications
- Speaking engagements, publications, media coverage

## AI-Assisted Content (Any Type)

### Disclosure Best Practices
- State *what* AI did, not just that AI was involved
  - Good: "AI was used to organize and structure findings from 12 customer interviews"
  - Weak: "This article was written with AI assistance"
- State *why* AI was used (efficiency, data processing, accessibility)
- Always include human expert review — and credit the reviewer

### What AI Should NOT Do Alone
- Make medical, legal, or financial recommendations
- Generate original research or data (it synthesizes existing data)
- Replace first-hand experience signals
- Stand in for a credentialed author on YMYL topics
